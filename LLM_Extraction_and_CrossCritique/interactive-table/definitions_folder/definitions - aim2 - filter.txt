ICI Class
 All - You can choose all the ICI classes
 PD1 - PD-1 is a receptor found on T cells (immune cells). It acts like a “brake” to prevent overactivation of the immune system.
 PD-L1 - PD-L1 is the ligand that binds to PD-1, found on tumor cells and some immune cells. Think of it as the “key” that turns off T cells when it fits into the PD-1 “lock.”
 CTLA-4 - CTLA-4 is another immune checkpoint receptor on T cells that competes with the stimulatory receptor CD28 for binding to B7 molecules (CD80/CD86) on antigen-presenting cells.

ICI Drug Definitions
Atezolizumab
Class: PD-L1 inhibitor
Mechanism: Blocks PD-L1 on tumor/immune cells → prevents PD-1-mediated T-cell inactivation.
Common Uses: Non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), triple-negative breast cancer, urothelial carcinoma.

Avelumab
Class: PD-L1 inhibitor
Mechanism: Similar to atezolizumab; also triggers antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC).
Common Uses: Merkel cell carcinoma, urothelial carcinoma, renal cell carcinoma (RCC).

Cemiplimab
Class: PD-1 inhibitor
Mechanism: Blocks PD-1 receptor on T cells to restore immune activity.
Common Uses: Cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma, basal cell carcinoma, NSCLC.

Durvalumab
Class: PD-L1 inhibitor
Mechanism: Blocks PD-L1 → T cells remain active against tumors.
Common Uses: Stage III NSCLC (consolidation after chemoradiation), extensive-stage small cell lung cancer.

Ipilimumab
Class: CTLA-4 inhibitor
Mechanism: Blocks CTLA-4 on T cells → enhances T-cell priming and activation in lymph nodes.
Common Uses: Melanoma, renal cell carcinoma (often in combination with nivolumab).

Nivolumab
Class: PD-1 inhibitor
Mechanism: Blocks PD-1 to restore T-cell function.
Common Uses: Melanoma, NSCLC, RCC, Hodgkin lymphoma, many others.

Pembrolizumab
Class: PD-1 inhibitor
Mechanism: Same as nivolumab; widely used as a single agent or in combination.
Common Uses: Multiple cancers including melanoma, NSCLC, head & neck squamous cell carcinoma, and MSI-H tumors.

Tremelimumab
Class: CTLA-4 inhibitor
Mechanism: Similar to ipilimumab; enhances early T-cell activation.
Common Uses: Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) and in combination regimens for other solid tumors.

Gastric/GEJ
Common ICIs: Pembrolizumab, Nivolumab

Head and Neck (HNSCC)
Common ICIs: Pembrolizumab, Nivolumab

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC)

Melanoma
Common ICIs: Nivolumab, Pembrolizumab, Ipilimumab, Tremelimumab

Type of Therapy

All
Meaning: Includes both monotherapy and combination therapy.
Use Case: When you want to see every trial or therapy option regardless of regimen.

Combination Therapy
Definition: The immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI) is used together with another therapy, such as:
Another ICI (e.g., Nivolumab + Ipilimumab)
Chemotherapy (e.g., Pembrolizumab + platinum-based chemo)
Targeted therapy or anti-angiogenic drugs (e.g., Atezolizumab + Bevacizumab in HCC)
Purpose: Combination therapy often increases response rates or broadens activity.

Monotherapy
Definition: The ICI is given alone, without any other cancer drug.
Examples:
Pembrolizumab monotherapy in MSI-H colorectal cancer
Nivolumab monotherapy in second-line NSCLC
Purpose: Used when the tumor is highly sensitive to immune activation (e.g., PD-L1 high or MSI-H tumors) or when combination therapy isn’t tolerated.

Type of combination (Treatment Arm)

All
Includes any combination or single-agent ICI.

ICI + ICI
Two immune checkpoint inhibitors together
Example:
Nivolumab (PD-1) + Ipilimumab (CTLA-4) in melanoma, RCC, mesothelioma
Goal: Synergistic immune activation by blocking multiple checkpoints.

ICI + Chemo
ICI combined with chemotherapy
Example:
Pembrolizumab + platinum-based chemo in NSCLC
Goal: Chemo reduces tumor bulk & can make tumor more “immunogenic,” enhancing ICI efficacy.

ICI + TKI (Tyrosine Kinase Inhibitor)
ICI with a targeted therapy that blocks oncogenic kinases
Example:
Pembrolizumab + Lenvatinib (RCC, endometrial cancer)
Goal: TKIs often inhibit VEGF or oncogenic pathways, modulating the tumor microenvironment.

ICI + MEKi (MEK Inhibitor)
ICI combined with a MEK pathway inhibitor
Example:
Investigational in melanoma with PD-1 + MEKi
Goal: MEK inhibition can make tumors more sensitive to immune attack.

ICI + Anti-VEGF
ICI combined with anti-angiogenic therapy (VEGF inhibitor)
Example:
Atezolizumab + Bevacizumab in HCC (IMbrave150)
Goal: Normalizes tumor vasculature and enhances immune infiltration.

ICI + RadiatICln (Radiation/Clinical Radiation)
ICI combined with radiotherapy
Example:
Pembrolizumab + SBRT in lung cancer trials
Goal: Radiation can trigger the abscopal effect, making tumors more immunogenic.

ICI + Vaccine
ICI combined with a cancer vaccine
Example:
Experimental: PD-1 inhibitor + peptide vaccine in melanoma
Goal: Vaccine primes T cells, ICI keeps them active.

ICI + BRAFi + MEKi
Triple therapy for BRAF-mutant melanoma
Example:
Dabrafenib (BRAFi) + Trametinib (MEKi) + Pembrolizumab
Goal: Combine targeted therapy and immunotherapy for deeper, more durable responses.

ICI + Chemo + Anti-VEGF
Triplet therapy: ICI + chemotherapy + VEGF inhibitor
Example:
Atezolizumab + Bevacizumab + Chemo in NSCLC
Goal: Attack tumor through immune, cytotoxic, and anti-angiogenic mechanisms simultaneously.

ICI + ICI + Chemo
Triple regimen: 2 ICIs + chemotherapy
Example:
Nivolumab + Ipilimumab + Chemo in NSCLC (CheckMate 9LA)
Goal: Maximal immune stimulation plus tumor cytoreduction

Control Arm Definitions in Clinical Trials

All
Includes any type of control arm.

Chemo
Standard chemotherapy alone (e.g., cisplatin, carboplatin, or taxanes depending on cancer type).

TKI (Tyrosine Kinase Inhibitor)
Targeted oral drugs that block kinase signaling pathways.
Example: Erlotinib (EGFR TKI in NSCLC).

Interferon
Immune-stimulating therapy historically used in melanoma and renal cell carcinoma before ICIs.

Multikinase inhibitor
Drugs that inhibit multiple kinases involved in tumor growth and angiogenesis.
Example: Sorafenib in hepatocellular carcinoma.

mTOR inhibitor
Drugs that block the mTOR pathway to slow cancer cell growth.
Example: Everolimus in RCC.

Radiation
Standard radiotherapy (local treatment to the tumor).

Vaccine
Experimental therapeutic cancer vaccine used as a comparator.

Placebo
Inert treatment; used in randomized controlled trials for unbiased comparison.

Chemo / Anti-EGFR
Combination of chemotherapy plus an anti-EGFR antibody.
Example: FOLFOX + Cetuximab in colorectal cancer.

Chemo / Anti-VEGF
Combination of chemotherapy plus an anti-angiogenic drug.
Example: Chemo + Bevacizumab in NSCLC.

Chemo / TKI
Combination of chemotherapy plus a tyrosine kinase inhibitor.

BRAFi + MEKi
Targeted therapy combination for BRAF-mutated melanoma.
Example: Dabrafenib (BRAFi) + Trametinib (MEKi).

Best Supportive Care (BSC)
Palliative care only; no active anticancer treatment.
Goal: Compare ICI to symptomatic management when no standard therapy exists.

Clinical Setting Definitions

All
Includes all treatment settings for analysis.

Adjuvant
Definition: Treatment given after primary therapy (usually surgery) to reduce the risk of recurrence.
Example ICIs:
Nivolumab for adjuvant melanoma or NSCLC
Pembrolizumab for adjuvant melanoma
Goal: Eliminate microscopic disease and prevent relapse.

Maintenance
Definition: Treatment given after initial therapy response to maintain disease control.
Example ICIs:
Durvalumab as maintenance after chemoradiation in Stage III NSCLC (PACIFIC trial)
Goal: Extend remission and delay progression.

Metastatic / Recurrent (Unresectable)
Definition: Treatment for advanced, unresectable, or metastatic cancer.
Example ICIs:
Pembrolizumab for metastatic NSCLC
Nivolumab + Ipilimumab for metastatic melanoma
Goal: Control or shrink tumors in incurable disease.

Neoadjuvant
Definition: Treatment given before surgery to shrink the tumor or prime the immune system.
Example ICIs:
Nivolumab ± Ipilimumab as neoadjuvant in NSCLC (CheckMate 816)
Goal: Improve surgical outcomes and reduce recurrence risk.

Trial Phase
All
Show trials regardless of phase.

Phase 2
Goal: signal-seeking—does the regimen look effective and reasonably safe?
Typical features: single-arm or randomized; endpoints like ORR, PFS, DoR; dozens to a few hundred patients. Positive Phase 2 → often moves to Phase 3.

Phase 3
Goal: confirm benefit vs a control arm and change practice if positive.
Typical features: randomized, larger sample; endpoints like OS, PFS, QoL; forms the basis for regulatory approvals and guidelines.

Type of Study
All
Show both original and subsequent reports.

Original publication
The first peer-reviewed paper reporting the trial’s primary analysis (pre-specified endpoints at the planned data cut). Think “main NEJM/JCO paper.”

Follow-up
Later analyses of the same trial: longer follow-up, updated OS/PFS, subgroup or biomarker analyses, safety updates, secondary endpoints, or post-hoc work. Useful for durability and late toxicities.

Primary endpoints (what each option means)
ORR (Objective Response Rate)
% of patients with tumor shrinkage meeting PR/CR by RECIST (or relevant criteria).

OS (Overall Survival)
Time from randomization/start to death from any cause.

PFS (Progression-Free Survival)
Time from start to disease progression or death, whichever comes first.

RFS (Recurrence-/Relapse-Free Survival)
Time after curative-intent therapy to first recurrence or death. (Often used interchangeably with DFS in adjuvant settings.)

Path CR (Pathologic Complete Response; pCR)
No residual viable tumor in resected specimen (primary ± nodes) after neoadjuvant therapy.

EFS (Event-Free Survival) (in combos like Path CR+EFS)
Time from randomization to a prespecified “event” (progression that precludes surgery, recurrence, second cancer, or death—definition varies by trial).

Safety
Primary endpoint focuses on adverse events (incidence/severity, e.g., grade ≥3, serious AEs, discontinuations).
